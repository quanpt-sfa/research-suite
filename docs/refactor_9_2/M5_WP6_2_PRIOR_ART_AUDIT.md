## 2. What each source changed in the design (and what it did not)

1. **Attribution is a separate axis from claim type** (SciArg's own/background split; SciFact's claims are reports about prior work). SciArg has no label for "the author criticises prior work" or for model/researcher interpretations; those two come from the owner's list.
2. **A claim needs several kinds of evidence link** (CLAIM-BENCH stores evidence as one text blob per claim; `one_to_many` is the claim → evidence structure in its tool). We store separate links, each with a role and the reason it is needed, and the character span of each.
3. **Recall costs tokens**: CLAIM-BENCH's three-pass and one-by-one conditions raise the number of retrieved pairs at a higher cost, and it does not report tokens. WP6.2 measures tokens and calls itself and does not assume that a cheaper strategy has equal coverage.
4. **Provenance of every statement** (ORKG `extractionMethod`, nanopub provenance). Research Suite already has `generated_by`, `origin` and run registry rows; the new records use them and add nothing parallel.
5. **Immutability with explicit retraction** (nanopub). Our store is append-only by revision; a withdrawn or superseded claim is a new revision with a state, never a deletion.

## 4. Limits of this audit

* I read the dataset cards and code, not every file. I did not read CLAIM-BENCH's notebooks, so how it matches predicted and gold spans is unverified here.
* SciArg's server being unreachable may be a property of this machine's network, not of the dataset; the researcher may be able to fetch it. Its licence still has to be established before any use.
* The ORKG running instance's data terms, the Dr. Inventor corpus terms and the S2ORC terms behind SciFact's abstracts were taken from the licence files only.
* GROBID was not run.
